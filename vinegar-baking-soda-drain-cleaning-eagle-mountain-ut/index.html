Vinegar and Baking Soda Drain Cleaning in Eagle Mountain, UT: What Works

Vinegar and baking soda can freshen a drain and loosen a thin film of soap or grease, but they rarely clear a real clog.

By the Eagle Mountain Pro Plumbing team · Published October 30, 2026 · Updated October 30, 2026

(385) 488-1892
Answered 24/7

Vinegar and baking soda can freshen a drain and loosen a thin film of soap or grease, but they rarely clear a real clog. The fizz is carbon dioxide gas plus water and sodium acetate, and it has very little force. Hair, a wad of food, or a stuck wipe will still be sitting there afterward.

Try it once on a slow drain. If the water stays high or the clog returns within days, a snake or a jetting job does the work the fizz cannot.

What actually happens when the foam starts

Baking soda is sodium bicarbonate, and white vinegar is about 5 percent acetic acid. When they meet, they react and release carbon dioxide. The bubbling looks dramatic, but the gas escapes up through the drain opening and does little to the blockage below.

What remains is mostly water with a little sodium acetate dissolved in it. That mixture is mild and safe for most household pipes in occasional use. It can lift a light film of soap scum and cut some odor from the trap.

It cannot dissolve hair, and it cannot push a dense plug through a bend. That limit explains why so many people pour a second round and see no change.

The process

The method, if you want to try it once

1

Clear the standing water

Bail out as much water as you can with a cup. Fizz needs to reach the clog, not float on top of a full basin.

2

Pour in the baking soda

Add about half a cup down the drain. Use a spoon to push it past the opening if it sits in the strainer.

3

Add the vinegar and cover the drain

Pour in about one cup of white vinegar and plug the opening. Plugging pushes more of the reaction down into the trap instead of up into your face.

4

Wait, then flush with hot tap water

Give it 15 to 30 minutes, then run hot water from the tap for a minute. If the drain still sits slow, do not repeat it more than once.

Skip these

Four add-ons that turn a mild routine into a mess

Some popular variations create problems. Leave them out.

Boiling water on plastic or porcelain

Rolling-boil water can soften PVC joints and crack a porcelain sink or tub drain. Hot tap water is enough.

Vinegar mixed with bleach

The combination releases chlorine gas. Never use the two in the same drain or the same hour.

Vinegar on top of a store drain cleaner

Leftover chemical can react with the vinegar and splash back. If you used a chemical product, tell your plumber before they open the trap.

Plunging after chemicals

A plunger can throw caustic liquid onto your skin and eyes. Wait for a plumber to deal with a drain that has chemical standing in it.

Local note

Eagle Mountain note: check your tap before you blame the drain

Eagle Mountain City Water Department supplies drinking water from five groundwater wells plus surface water from the Central Utah Water Conservancy District. The city's annual drinking water quality report does not publish a hardness figure. If you see white crusty scale around faucets or showerheads, test your tap, or ask the Water Department for its annual drinking water quality report.

Scale on an aerator or showerhead is a different problem from a clog in the drain. Vinegar can soften that scale, but a soak in a bag is the better use than pouring it down the pipe. A drain that is slow deep in the line still needs a cable or a jetter.

Worth knowing

Why hair beats the fizz every time

Hair is a protein, and vinegar and baking soda do not break proteins down. A hair clump also knots into soap and grease, which makes a tough, rubbery plug. Water slips around the edges, so the drain still seems to run slowly. Only mechanical removal gets it out, which means a hook, a hand snake, or a power cable that grabs it.

Checklist

Signs it is time to stop pouring and phone a plumber

One round of fizz is a fair test. After that, treat these signs as a reason to stop.

  • ✓Water still stands in the sink or tub after the flush.
  • ✓The same drain slows down again within a week.
  • ✓Two or more fixtures drain slowly at the same time.
  • ✓You hear gurgling from a toilet when another drain runs.
  • ✓Water or sewage backs up in a floor drain or the lowest tub.
  • ✓A drain smells like sewage even after cleaning.
  • ✓You already used a chemical opener and the clog remains.

What a plumber does that a pantry mix cannot

A professional starts by finding where the blockage sits, since a clog in the trap acts differently from one deeper in the line. Then the plumber picks a cable, an auger, or a water jet to match the material. Our drain cleaning service covers sinks, tubs, showers, and floor drains with that approach.

If the line clogs again weeks later, grease or roots are the usual reason, and a cable only punches a hole through them. In that case hydro jetting scours the pipe wall with high-pressure water. Ask about it if you keep having the same problem.

Small habits that do more than a monthly fizz

Put a hair catcher over every tub and shower drain and empty it after use. Scrape plates into the trash before washing, and keep cooking grease in a can instead of the sink. Run hot tap water for a minute after washing greasy dishes.

Grease cools inside the pipe and sticks to the wall, which narrows the passage a little with every meal. A strainer and a habit cost almost nothing and prevent more clogs than any pantry recipe.

Questions readers ask

Is vinegar and baking soda safe for every pipe?

In occasional use it is mild enough for PVC, ABS, copper, and porcelain. The risk comes from boiling water and from mixing it with bleach or leftover drain cleaner. If you are unsure what is in the drain, stop and ask a plumber.

Why does my drain smell better but still drain slowly?

The fizz can lift odor from the trap, but a slow drain usually means something is narrowing the pipe. Hair, grease, or a soft plug further down can survive the treatment. Slow after cleaning means mechanical clearing is next.

Can I use it in a kitchen sink with a garbage disposal?

Yes, in small amounts. Run cold water afterward and do not pour boiling water into the disposal. Grease and food packed past the disposal need a cable, not more fizz.

How often can I repeat the vinegar and baking soda treatment?

Try it once, and then stop if nothing changes. Repeated rounds waste time and delay the real fix. A drain that slows again within days needs a different tool.

Do homes in Eagle Mountain have any special drain issues?

Most homes here were built since the late 1990s, so the drain lines are newer than in older towns, and age-related collapse is less likely. The common causes are the usual ones: hair, grease, soap, and wipes. Newer pipes still clog, so a repeat clog is worth a look.
