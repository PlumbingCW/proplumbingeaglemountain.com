What a sewer camera inspection shows

A sewer inspection in Eagle Mountain, UT sends a small waterproof camera through your main sewer line, so you can see its condition on a live screen. Sewer camera inspection is the video examination of the pipe between the cleanout and the street tie-in, using a camera on a flexible push rod.

It shows cracks, roots, sags, offset joints and buildup, and a locator tracks the camera head to mark depth and position. You learn the facts before a purchase, a repair or a landscaping project.

The process

What happens during the camera run

1

Find the entry

We locate your main cleanout. With no cleanout, we pull a toilet or use a roof vent.

2

Push the camera

A flexible rod feeds the camera head through the line while the monitor records. Lights and a self-leveling head keep the picture upright.

3

Mark depth and location

A locator above ground picks up the camera's transmitter, so we can flag the position and depth of each problem.

4

Review the video together

We replay anything unusual with you and explain what it means. If cleaning or repair is needed, you hear the clear price before that work starts.

When to inspect

Good reasons to scope your sewer line

Most homeowners schedule a camera run for one of these six reasons.

Before buying a home

A standard home inspection may not cover the sewer lateral, so a camera run answers what the walk-through cannot.

Slow drains that return

Repeat clogs often point at a sag, an offset or roots that a camera confirms.

After a backup

A camera shows whether a one-time clog was a plug or a broken pipe.

Soft or sunken yard spots

Wet ground over the line may mean a leak or a collapse.

Before digging or landscaping

Knowing the depth and path of the pipe keeps shovels, trees and fence posts off it. Call 811 as well.

A newer home with odd symptoms

Most Eagle Mountain homes are fairly new, but a young line can still have poor bedding, a loose joint or leftover construction debris.

Worth knowing

What a locator adds to the picture

A camera shows what the pipe looks like. A locator shows where the camera is. The camera head carries a small transmitter, called a sonde, and a receiver above ground reads its position and depth.

That matters when a break needs repair, because the crew can dig a small, exact pit instead of trenching the yard. It also keeps you from planting over the line. Before any dig, call 811 (Blue Stakes of Utah) so other buried utilities are marked.

Questions about sewer camera inspection in Eagle Mountain

What does a sewer camera inspection cost in Eagle Mountain?

The price depends on access, line length and whether cleaning is needed first. Call (385) 488-1892 and you will hear a clear price before work starts.

How long does a sewer camera inspection take?

The camera run is usually short for a standard house line, and locating and review add time. Access, cleaning and line length change that, so ask when you book.

Do I need a sewer inspection when buying a home in Eagle Mountain?

Many homes here are newer, which lowers some risks but not all. A standard home inspection may not cover the sewer lateral, so a camera run is a reasonable add-on, mainly if the seller cannot show the line's history.

Can the camera find a clog or a leak?

It finds the physical cause of a clog, such as roots or a sag, and it shows a leak in the pipe wall if it appears as a crack. A wet spot with no visible pipe damage may call for leak detection.

What if my house is on septic?

A camera can inspect the house line up to the tank. Tanks and drain fields work under separate rules, so ask the city or county which system serves your lot.

Will you dig if you find a problem?

Not without your say. We explain the video, then lay out options such as cleaning, lining or sewer line repair, and you hear the clear price first.

Do I need a permit for a sewer inspection?

A camera inspection is not a repair, so it usually does not need one. Repairs might. Permit rules vary, so ask the Eagle Mountain Building Department at 801-789-6614.
