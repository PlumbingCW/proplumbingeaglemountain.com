Hydro Jetting a Sewer Line: A Homeowner Guide for Eagle Mountain, UT

Hydro jetting cleans a sewer line with a hose and a nozzle that shoots pressurized water against the pipe wall.

By the Eagle Mountain Pro Plumbing team · Published October 30, 2026 · Updated October 30, 2026

Talk to a plumber
(385) 488-1892
Answered 24/7

Hydro jetting cleans a sewer line with a hose and a nozzle that shoots pressurized water against the pipe wall. The water scrubs off grease, sludge, and root hair instead of punching one hole through the clog. Homeowners in Eagle Mountain, UT usually look at it after a snake got stuck, after a clog came back within weeks, or after roots grew back.

Jetting works best on pipe that is still intact. A camera look first tells the plumber whether the line can take the pressure.

What the water actually does inside the pipe

A snake, also called a cable, bores a round hole through a blockage. The rest of the buildup stays on the wall, so the hole slowly closes again. That is why a drain can run fine for a month and then back up once more.

A jetting nozzle works differently. Its forward jet breaks up the blockage, and its rear jets pull the hose along while they scour the full circle of the pipe. A good pass leaves a clean wall, not just a clear center.

The tool does not care much about what built up. Grease, soap scum, wipes, and fine silt all wash toward the city main once the water has broken them loose.

Snake, jetter, or camera first: how the three compare

Each tool answers a different question, and the right order often saves a second visit.

Symptoms

Slow drain that keeps coming back: signs jetting may fit

Here is how homeowners often describe the problem, matched to what may be happening in the line.

The snake got stuck

A cable that hangs up often meets roots, a rough joint, or a thick plug of grease. Jetting may clear it, but a camera should confirm the pipe is not collapsed first.

Backups every few weeks

A short-lived fix suggests buildup on the wall. A full-circle scrub tends to last longer than a single hole through the middle.

Gurgling from a tub or floor drain

Air pushed back through other drains can point to a partial blockage in the main. Several fixtures gurgling at once is a main line sign.

Water pooling around a basement floor drain

If the lowest drain in the house fills first, the main line may be restricted. Stop running water and have the line checked.

Roots showing on the camera

Roots enter at joints and crack lines. Jetting can cut and flush soft roots, but it does not fix the opening they came through.

Grease heard rattling in the line

Kitchen grease cools and hardens downstream. Jetting is a common choice for that kind of coating.

The process

What a jetting visit looks like from start to finish

1

Locate the access point

The plumber finds a cleanout, usually a capped pipe outside near the foundation or inside near the main. No cleanout means a toilet may need to be pulled, which adds work.

2

Run a camera

A small camera shows pipe material, joints, roots, and any sag. Cracked or collapsed pipe changes the plan before any pressure goes in.

3

Feed the hose and jet in passes

The hose goes in with the jets firing backward. The plumber works down the line, then pulls back slowly to scour the wall on the return trip.

4

Camera the line again

A second look confirms the wall is clean and shows whether roots, a bellied section, or a bad joint still need repair.

Worth knowing

Why flow rate matters as much as pressure

Pressure is measured in PSI, and flow is measured in gallons per minute. High pressure with weak flow cuts well but does not carry debris far. A strong jetter pairs both, so loosened material rides the water down to the city main instead of settling a few feet away. When you compare services, ask what machine will be used and what nozzle fits a line of your pipe diameter.

Side by side

When jetting is the right call, and when it is not

Jetting usually fits

  • Grease and sludge coating in a sound pipe
  • Repeat clogs with no visible damage
  • Soft root growth after the pipe was cut back
  • Regular maintenance on a restaurant style heavy use line

Look at repair instead

  • Cracked, offset, or collapsed sections on camera
  • A bellied low spot that holds standing water
  • Old, brittle pipe that high pressure could damage
  • Roots that return fast because a joint has failed

Keeping the line clear after the visit

Jetting resets the pipe, but habits decide how long the reset lasts. Wipes labeled flushable still tangle in a main line, so only toilet paper should go down the toilet. Pour cooled cooking grease into a can, not the sink.

If roots were the cause, ask about a camera check on a set schedule. Catching regrowth early is easier than clearing a full blockage. For a deeper explanation of the service itself, see our hydro jetting service page.

Why a camera look pairs with the jet

High pressure inside a damaged pipe can make a small crack bigger. Pipe camera footage shows the wall before the nozzle goes in, so the plumber can decide whether to jet or recommend repair first.

Questions readers ask

Will hydro jetting damage my sewer pipe?

On sound pipe it is generally considered safe when the operator uses the right nozzle and pressure. Damaged or very old pipe is a different story, which is why a camera check usually comes first.

How is jetting different from snaking a main line?

A snake cuts a path through the middle of a blockage. A jetter scours the whole pipe wall, so it removes the buildup that lets clogs return.

Does jetting kill tree roots for good?

No. It cuts and flushes the roots inside the pipe, but they can grow back through the same opening. A cracked joint needs repair if regrowth is fast.

Do I need a cleanout for jetting?

A cleanout makes it far easier. Without one, a plumber may need to remove a toilet to reach the line, which adds time.

How long should a clean line stay clear?

There is no fixed answer because it depends on grease habits, roots, and pipe condition. Ask the plumber to show before and after camera footage so you can judge.

What should a main line backup in Eagle Mountain, UT make me do first?

Stop running water, since every flush and shower adds to the overflow. Then call (385) 488-1892 for dispatch, and ask a licensed plumber to check the line with a camera before choosing jetting or repair.
