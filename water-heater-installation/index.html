Water heater installation in Eagle Mountain, UT, done on your timeline

A planned water heater installation in Eagle Mountain, UT starts with a site visit to the room where the tank will live. We look at the gas line, the flue, the electrical panel, the drain, and the floor. That decides what can go in without rework.

Water heater installation means setting a new heater in a new or existing spot, then connecting water, fuel or power, venting, relief valve, and an expansion tank where the plumbing calls for one. Same-day visits for site checks are possible when openings exist.

Permits, inspections, and who to ask in Eagle Mountain

On the city's what-requires-a-permit sheet, water heaters sit with the plumbing work that needs a permit unless specifically exempted. Worth knowing

Why a closed plumbing system needs an expansion tank

Water expands as it heats, and a tank full of hot water has nowhere to send the extra volume. When a check valve or pressure reducing valve closes the line, that pressure has no way back to the street. It lands on the relief valve, the tank, and every faucet in the house.

A small expansion tank with an air cushion absorbs the extra volume. It also protects the valves and connectors on a newer heater. We check your house pressure before we pick one.

Vent types

Venting a new heater, from flue pipes to PVC runs

The vent is the part most often wrong on a rushed job. Each style has its own pipe, slope, and clearance rules, which follow the manufacturer's instructions and local code. A tankless unit adds its own demands, covered under tankless water heaters.

Natural draft flue

Hot exhaust rises through a metal vent to the roof. It needs slope, correct sizing, and enough room air.

Power vent

A fan pushes exhaust out through plastic pipe, so the run can go sideways through a wall. It needs a nearby outlet.

Direct vent

Sealed combustion draws outdoor air in and sends exhaust out, so it works in tight rooms.

Condensing models

Very efficient gas heaters cool exhaust so much that condensate forms, which needs PVC pipe and a drain.

Electric tank

No flue at all, only a dedicated circuit and a breaker sized for the elements.

Heat pump tank

No flue, but it needs open air space and a condensate drain, and it cools the room it sits in.

Questions about water heater installation in Eagle Mountain

Do I need a permit to install a water heater in Eagle Mountain?

Water heaters are on the city's permit list. Some like for like work can be exempt, and permit rules vary, so ask the Eagle Mountain Building Department at 801-789-6614. We work with the inspection when one is required.

Does a new water heater need an expansion tank?

If a check valve or pressure reducing valve makes your plumbing a closed system, an expansion tank is the standard fix. We read your pressure and decide on site.

Can the heater go in the attic or a closet?

Often yes, with a drain pan, a drain route, venting, and air. An attic leak has a long way to fall, so we are careful about those.

Can you convert from gas to electric during installation?

That depends on the panel and the available circuits. Gas to electric needs a dedicated 240 volt circuit. Electric to gas needs a gas line and a vent. We check both.

What does venting involve for a gas heater?

The vent must slope, stay the right size, and keep clear of combustibles. Power vent and direct vent models use plastic pipe through a wall, while natural draft units use a metal flue to the roof.

Can I install a tankless unit instead of a tank?

Yes, but the gas line, venting, and wiring often need upgrades. Read about tankless water heaters and ask for an assessment first.

What happens to the old water heater?

We drain it, disconnect it, and haul it out if you ask. If it is leaking or has failed, see water heater repair first to see if a part can save it.
