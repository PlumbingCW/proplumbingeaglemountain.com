Oquirrh Mountain Ranch is a newer residential area in Eagle Mountain, and its streets are younger than most of the city. A house this recent can still develop a leaking supply line, a running toilet or a water heater that stops heating. Call (385) 488-1892 and tell dispatch your street.

A note on boundaries first. The city treats this area as the southernmost parcel of The Ranches master development, so it overlaps with the larger Ranches name. We keep a separate page because people search for the subdivision name on its own.

What plumbing looks like in a newer home

Homes built after 2015 are mostly under a decade old, so failures tend to come from single parts and not from worn-out systems. The usual calls are a toilet that keeps running, a faucet that drips at the base, a loose supply connection under a sink or a water heater that trips or goes cold.

Before paying for a repair, ask whether a builder or manufacturer warranty still covers the part. Many repairs on a newer house are small, and our toilet repair and faucet repair pages explain what usually fails.

What to watch

Small problems that grow in newer homes

These are general plumbing patterns and not findings about any one street.

A toilet that refills on its own

A worn flapper or fill valve lets water leak into the bowl. It wastes water quietly and is cheap to fix early.

Damp cabinet floors

A slow drip at a supply connection rots cabinet bottoms before you see a puddle. Touch the fittings with a dry paper towel.

Hot water that goes cold fast

A failed element, thermostat or gas control often causes it. See water heater repair before replacing a young tank.

The process

If you find water under a sink

1

Close the fixture valve

Turn the small oval handle on the supply line clockwise. If it will not move, close the main instead.

2

Dry and look

Wipe every fitting, then watch for the first bead of water to return and note where.

3

Photograph it

Take a clear photo of the wet spot and the fitting so the plumber knows the part before arriving.

4

Call with details

Dial (385) 488-1892 and describe the fixture, the location and what you have already closed.

Questions from Oquirrh Mountain Ranch homeowners

Is Oquirrh Mountain Ranch part of The Ranches?

City records call it the southernmost parcel of The Ranches master development. Residents often use the subdivision name by itself, so give dispatch both your street and the name you use.

Will my builder warranty still apply to a plumbing repair?

Possibly, depending on the part and the warranty terms. Read your closing paperwork or ask the builder before authorizing a repair, then tell the plumber what you learned.

How many homes are in the subdivision?

We cannot say. City documents allow up to 417 units and a news report cited fewer, and neither tells us how many are built today.

What should I check about a plumber before they start?

Run the name through the Utah Verify a License lookup to see class and status, and ask for the number up front.
