Basement waterproofing in Eagle Mountain, UT, and what it means

Basement waterproofing is the set of steps that keep water out of a lower level or move it away safely. In Eagle Mountain, UT, the need for any of it depends on your own home, since not every house has a basement and builders vary. If your home has a lower level that feels damp, the first job is finding where the water comes from.

We handle the plumbing side, such as sump pits, pumps, floor drains, and pipe leaks. For structural crack repair or exterior excavation, we will tell you when another trade should lead.

Side by side

Interior drainage or exterior fixes, honest trade-offs

Interior drainage and pump

  • Collects water at the footing or slab and sends it to a sump pit
  • Works inside, with less digging around the yard
  • Needs a pump that stays powered and maintained
  • Manages water, and does not stop it reaching the wall

Exterior work and surface drainage

  • Gutters, downspout extensions, and grading move water away
  • Excavation and membranes seal the outside of the wall
  • Excavation affects landscaping and may need specialist crews
  • Call 811 before any digging so buried lines are marked

Rule out a pipe before you pay for waterproofing

Many damp lower levels turn out to be plumbing. A pinhole in a copper line, a failing washing machine hose, or a leaking drain can soak a wall for weeks. A quick test is to turn off every fixture and watch the water meter. If it keeps moving, you have a leak in the pipes, and leak detection finds it.

If the meter stands still and the wall stays wet after rain or snowmelt, the water is coming from outside. That is a drainage question, and the next steps are gutters, grading, and possibly a sump system.

Worth knowing

A backwater valve and a sump pump do different jobs

A sump pump handles groundwater collected in a pit and pushes it outside. A backwater valve, also called a backflow valve, sits in the sewer line and has a flap that closes if sewage flows backward toward the house. If a floor drain is the lowest opening in the building, a backwater valve may be worth discussing. See sump pump service for the pump side.

Questions about basement waterproofing in Eagle Mountain

Do all Eagle Mountain homes have basements?

No. Many houses have one, others have slab or crawlspace construction, and builders vary. Check your own home. If you have a lower level that feels damp, a plumber can tell whether pipes, drains, or surface water are involved.

Is a wet basement always a plumbing problem?

No. Pipe leaks, appliance failures, and drain backups are plumbing. Water from rain or snowmelt reaching a wall is drainage, and gutters and grading come first.

How can I tell if the water is from a leaking pipe?

Turn off all fixtures and watch the meter for fifteen minutes. If it moves, there is a leak somewhere in the water lines. A moving meter calls for leak detection.

What does a sump pump do for a wet basement?

It moves water from a pit to the outside. It helps only if water reaches the pit through drain tile or a floor opening. It does not seal walls or cracks.

Do I need a permit for basement drainage work?

It depends on the work. Permit rules vary, so ask the Eagle Mountain Building Department at 801-789-6614. Plumbing work such as drains and sewer connections is on the city's list of permitted work, with exemptions.

Should I call 811 before work near the foundation?

Yes. Call 811 (Blue Stakes of Utah) before any digging so buried utility lines are marked.
