When the water line under your yard fails

A failed water service line in Eagle Mountain, UT is urgent because the leak runs all day and night under your yard. Dispatch is 24/7, and while you wait you should close the main valve at the house and stop using water.

A water service line is the buried pipe that carries drinking water from the utility connection at the meter to the main valve inside your home. Repairing it means finding the break, digging only where needed and restoring a pipe that holds pressure.

Urgent

Water surfacing in the yard or no water at all

Bubbling water, a sinking patch of lawn or a sudden loss of all water points to a serious line failure. We dispatch 24/7, and same-day appointments are offered when available for slow leaks that can wait for daylight.

Symptoms of a failing service line

Wet lawn, weak shower and a meter that keeps turning

Most people describe this as a yard or pressure problem. The technical name is a water service line leak, and the symptoms below are how it usually announces itself.

Soggy patch with no rain

Water finds the easiest path up from a break, so a green or squishy spot can sit well away from the damage.

Pressure drop at every tap

When water escapes before reaching the house, all fixtures lose force at once.

Meter turns with the house valve closed

That points to a leak between the meter and the home.

Sound of running water outside

A hiss or rush near the foundation can come from a pressurized line breaking.

Bill jumped without a reason

A buried leak can run for weeks before the surface shows anything.

Dirty or cloudy water

A break can pull soil into the line when pressure drops. Flush a tap and call if it persists.

The line from the meter to your front door

Water enters from the street main, passes through the meter and then runs underground to a shutoff valve inside your home. Common service line pipe is copper or plastic tubing. Each material fails differently, and a plumber can tell which you have by looking at the entry point.

Ask the Water Department where the utility's side ends and the homeowner's side begins before any digging. That tells you what is yours to repair, and we help sort it out during the first visit.

Buried leak repair: how we find it and how little we dig

We confirm the line is the problem first. A meter test and a pressure test separate a buried line leak from a plumbing leak inside the home. Then we listen along the line to narrow the search to a short stretch of ground.

Call 811 before any digging so buried gas, power and telecom lines are marked. Once the break is exposed, the plumber cuts out the bad section and joins new pipe with proper fittings, or replaces the entire line if it is worn or repeatedly leaking. Where the layout allows, trenchless methods can reduce lawn damage, and we explain the options before you pick.

Low water pressure that comes from the supply side

Low pressure is not always a leak. A pressure regulator that has failed, a partly closed main valve or a clogged strainer can all starve the house. Start by opening the main valve all the way and checking whether the problem hits every fixture or only one.

A pressure gauge on an outdoor hose bib shows static pressure. Many plumbers treat anything above 80 psi as too high for household fixtures, and a weak reading with the valves open points toward a leak or a failing regulator. We test both before recommending a repair, because replacing a line to fix a bad regulator wastes money.

Your main shutoff valve is part of the repair

The main shutoff valve is the one you turn when anything bursts, so it has to work. Older gate valves seize with mineral buildup and can break when forced. Newer quarter-turn ball valves are simpler and far less likely to stick.

If the main valve will not close, we replace it as part of the line repair. Doing it while the water is already off saves a second visit. Practice turning your valve once a year so you know it moves.

Side by side

Repair a section or replace the whole line

A section repair fits when

  • The break is a single fitting or a short split
  • The rest of the pipe tests sound under pressure
  • The line is relatively new
  • You want the smallest trench possible

Full replacement fits when

  • Leaks keep appearing in different places
  • The pipe is kinked, crushed or visibly corroded
  • A section repair would cost nearly as much as new pipe
  • You are already trenching for another project

Questions about water service line repair in Eagle Mountain

Do you handle water service line repair emergencies at night or on weekends in Eagle Mountain?

Yes. Dispatch runs 24/7. Close the main shutoff valve in the house, then call (385) 488-1892. Same-day appointments are offered when available for slow leaks that can wait until morning.

How can I tell if my service line is leaking or the leak is inside the house?

Close the main valve at the house and check the meter. If the dial still turns, the leak is in the buried line. If it stops, the leak is inside the home. See leak detection for the full test.

Why is my water pressure suddenly low?

A line leak, a failed regulator, a half closed main valve or a clogged fixture can all cause it. If every tap is weak, we test the line and the regulator. If only one tap is weak, clean the aerator first.

Will you dig up my whole yard?

No. We locate the break first and dig only that area. A longer trench is needed only if the line must be replaced.

Who is responsible for the line, me or the city?

Where the utility's responsibility ends varies, so check with the Eagle Mountain City Water Department. Anything on the street side is the city's concern.

What about a water main replacement?

Water main replacement is utility work done by the city, not a homeowner plumbing job. We handle the private line from the meter to your house.

Does a service line repair need a permit?

Permit rules vary, so ask the Eagle Mountain Building Department at 801-789-6614. The city's permit sheet lists water supply piping, with some minor repairs possibly exempt.

Where the rules come from
