When to Replace an Aging Water Heater in Eagle Mountain, UT

Replace a water heater when it reaches the end of its typical life and starts to show leaks, rust, loud noises, or repeat repairs.

By the Eagle Mountain Pro Plumbing team · Published October 30, 2026 · Updated October 30, 2026

Answered 24/7

Replace a water heater when it reaches the end of its typical life and starts to show leaks, rust, loud noises, or repeat repairs. The US Department of Energy says most storage water heaters last 10 to 15 years, and tankless units can last more than 20.

Eagle Mountain homes are newer than most, so a 30-year-old heater would be a rarity here. You can still learn your heater's real age in two minutes by reading the serial number on its data plate. The age plus the symptoms decide whether you repair or replace.

Why a 30-year-old water heater is unlikely in Eagle Mountain

Articles about aging water heaters often picture a basement tank from the 1980s. That picture does not fit here. Eagle Mountain was incorporated in 1996, so even the oldest homes date to the late 1990s.

A heater that was never replaced in one of those homes would be close to 30 years old at the very oldest. Most have probably been swapped once already. The more useful question for your home is whether a 10 to 20 year old tank is nearing the end, and your serial number answers that.

The process

How to read your water heater's age from the serial number

1

Find the data plate

Look for a silver or white sticker on the side of the tank, usually a few feet up from the floor. It lists the model, capacity, and serial number.

2

Locate the serial number

It is a string of letters and digits, often labeled SER or Serial No. Take a photo with your phone so you can zoom in.

3

Decode the date

Many brands start the serial number with a letter for the month and two digits for the year. Formats differ by brand and by year, so treat any pattern as a clue, not proof.

4

Confirm with the maker or a plumber

Manufacturers publish decoding guides, and a plumber who sees the model can usually read it on the spot. Remember the code shows when the tank was built, not the day it was installed.

These bands are a rule of thumb built around the Department of Energy's 10 to 15 year range. A single unit can fall outside them.

Watch for these

Six warning signs that a tank is on its way out

Age tells you when to start paying attention. These symptoms tell you the tank is actually failing.

Water at the base of the tank

A puddle may come from a fitting or from the tank seam. If the tank body is wet, the heater is done.

Rust-colored hot water

Brown water from only the hot taps points to corrosion inside the tank, not the pipes.

Rumbling or popping from the tank

Sediment trapped under the burner or element boils and pops. It wastes energy and stresses the tank floor.

Relief valve that drips

A dripping temperature and pressure relief valve can signal excess pressure or a worn valve. It needs a plumber soon.

Hot water runs out faster

A thick layer of sediment takes up room in the tank and leaves less space for heated water.

Repairs stacking up

A second or third repair in a short span means the heater is wearing out in several places at once.

Worth knowing

What to do when a tank leaks, and why it cannot be repaired

A storage heater is a steel tank with a glass-like lining and a sacrificial anode rod that corrodes first to protect the steel. Once the anode is used up, rust begins to eat through the tank wall from the inside. Sealing the outside does nothing, because the damage is behind the insulation and the lining.

Leaks at a fitting, a drain valve, or a relief valve are different. Those are repairable, which is why a plumber checks exactly where the water comes from before giving a verdict.

If water is coming from the tank itself, switch off the power at the breaker or turn the gas control to pilot or off, then close the cold supply valve above the heater. Open a hot tap to relieve pressure and put a bucket under any drip. Then call the 24/7 line, because a tank that has started to leak through the wall will not stop.

Side by side

Repair or replace: the deciding factors

Lean toward repair

  • The tank is dry and the failed part is small
  • The heater is under about 10 years old
  • This is the first repair in a long time
  • The tank lining and anode have been checked

Lean toward replacement

  • Water is coming from the tank body
  • The heater is at 10 to 15 years or beyond
  • Repairs are repeating within a short span
  • Venting or gas connections need corrections

Planned replacement versus waiting for the leak

A tank that fails in a utility closet or finished space can soak flooring and drywall before anyone notices. A planned swap lets you pick the model, schedule the work, and shut the water off on your terms. If your heater is in the replacement window, read through the water heater replacement process before it fails.

If the heater is younger and only a part has failed, a water heater repair visit may buy years of service. The plumber's job is to tell you honestly which side of the line you are on.

Questions readers ask

How long does a water heater last?

The US Department of Energy says most storage water heaters last 10 to 15 years, while tankless units can last more than 20. Water conditions, maintenance, and usage all shift those numbers.

Can I tell my heater's age without a serial number?

Sometimes. Check closing paperwork, a permit record, or a service sticker near the unit. The data plate is still the best source. If it is faded, a plumber may know the maker's date code from the model.

Should I replace a water heater before it leaks?

If it is past 10 to 15 years or showing rust, noise, or a dripping relief valve, planning ahead is wise. A planned swap avoids water damage and gives you time to choose the right size and type.

Is it worth flushing an old tank to extend its life?

A flush clears loose sediment and can help a tank in its middle years. On a very old tank, it may not extend life, and heavy sediment can make the drain valve clog. Ask a plumber to look first.

Do I need a permit to replace my water heater in Eagle Mountain?

The city's permit sheet lists water heaters as work that needs a permit unless specifically exempted. Rules vary by job, so ask the Eagle Mountain Building Department at 3267 E Campus Dr, Suite H, or call 801-789-6614 before the work starts.
