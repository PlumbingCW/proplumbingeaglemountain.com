A sump pump is a submersible pump set in a pit that collects groundwater or drain water and sends it outside. In Eagle Mountain, UT we repair and replace sump pumps, float switches, and check valves, and install new ones where a pit exists.

My basement has water: is that a sump pump problem?

People describe this problem in many ways: water coming up through the floor, a damp corner, a pit that fills and never empties, or a pump that hums but moves nothing. These all point to a sump or drainage question, and the technical names are sump pump, ejector pump, and floor drain.

A sump pump moves water from a pit that collects drain tile or runoff. An ejector pump moves wastewater from a basement bathroom up to the sewer line. If your home has a pit, we can tell which one you have in a single visit. If it has neither, ask about waterproofing for a wet space.

When a full pit and a dead pump cannot wait

If water is rising toward the floor, call. A pump that runs nonstop, a pit that overflows, or a pump that will not start can flood a finished space. Night calls reach dispatch, and a same-day visit may fit if the schedule has room.

  4. If the water is sewage from a bathroom ejector, stop using that bathroom and tell us on the phone.

Worth knowing

The check valve is the cheapest part that saves the most

A check valve sits on the discharge pipe and stops the water in the line from falling back into the pit after each cycle. Without one, the pump re-pumps the same water, wears faster, and short cycles. A good check valve has a spring or flapper that closes without slamming. Replacing it every few years is simple maintenance and costs far less than a new pump.

The process

How we test and replace a pump

1

Power down and inspect

We cut power, remove the lid, and look at the pit, float, cord, and discharge connection.

2

Test the float and the check valve

We pour water into the pit to see the pump cycle and listen for rapid cycling or slamming.

3

Remove and replace

A failed pump is lifted out, the new one is set level on a solid base, and the float is positioned clear of the walls.

4

Confirm discharge

We run water through the cycle, check the outdoor discharge, and make sure it drains away from the foundation.

Checklist

A simple pump check for homeowners

Do this a couple of times a year, especially before spring thaw and heavy rain. It takes about ten minutes.

  • ✓Look at the discharge outlet outside for ice, debris, or a crushed pipe

Questions about sump pump repair and installation in Eagle Mountain

Do you handle sump pump emergencies at night or on weekends in Eagle Mountain?

Yes. Dispatch is staffed every hour, and sump pump calls on nights, weekends, and holidays go to dispatched plumbers. Check the power and the float while you wait. Same-day work is offered when our schedule allows.

How do I know if my home even has a sump pump?

Look at the lowest level for a round pit with a lid, or a pipe leading outside. Not every home has one. If you are unsure, a plumber can check on a visit.

What does it mean if my pump runs all the time?

The float may be stuck, the pump may be undersized, or water may keep entering the pit. A plumber checks the float and check valve first, then looks at the source of the water.

Is a sump pump the same as an ejector pump?

No. A sump pump moves groundwater or drain tile water out of a pit. An ejector pump lifts wastewater from a basement bathroom or laundry to the sewer line. They look similar but have different rules.

Do I need a battery backup?

If a power outage leaves you without a working pump and water can reach finished space, a battery backup is worth discussing. It is an added system, and we explain what it does and does not cover.

Does a sump pump fix a wet basement?

Only if the water collects in a pit. If water seeps through walls or cracks, you may need drainage or waterproofing work, and we tell you honestly which fits.

How long does a sump pump last?

Many last several years, but it depends on the model and how often it runs. A pump that is noisy, cycles oddly, or is quite old is a candidate for replacement before it fails.
