Urgent

When an overflowing or leaking toilet is an emergency

Call right away if the bowl is spilling over, water is pooling around the base, or the tank is dripping through a ceiling below. Water that touches drywall or flooring keeps doing damage until the supply is off.

  1. Close the angle stop on the wall by turning it clockwise until it stops.
  2. If the bowl is rising, lift the tank lid and push the flapper down or hold the float arm up.
  3. Lay towels around the base and keep children and pets out of the bathroom.
  4. Do not flush again, even to test, until a plumber has looked at the drain.
  5. If several drains are gurgling or backing up, stop using all water and mention it when you call.

Common symptoms

What a failing toilet is telling you

Most toilet complaints fall into a handful of patterns. Matching yours to one of these speeds up the phone call and the repair.

Handle jiggles to stop the sound

The flapper is not sealing or the chain is too long, so water leaks from the tank into the bowl all day.

Water around the base

A failed wax ring, loose closet bolts, or a sweating tank can all put water on the floor, and each has a different fix.

Bowl rocks when you sit

The flange or bolts have loosened, which will eventually break the wax seal and leak sewage gas into the room.

Weak or slow flush

Mineral buildup in the rim jets, a low tank water level, or a partial clog in the trap can all thin out the flush.

Hissing after the tank fills

A worn fill valve is letting water creep past its seat, and the valve never fully closes.

Crack in the porcelain

A hairline crack in the tank or bowl will grow, and no sealant gives a lasting repair, so replacement is the real fix.

Running toilet, leaking toilet: where the water is really going

A running toilet can waste a surprising amount of water without any visible mess. The loss is quiet because it runs down the bowl wall into the drain. A few drops of food coloring in the tank will show it: if color reaches the bowl within ten minutes without a flush, the flapper or seat is leaking.

A leaking toilet is a different problem. Water at the floor usually comes from the supply line, the shutoff valve, the tank bolts, or the wax ring beneath the bowl. Dry every joint with a paper towel and watch which one wets first.

If the toilet checks out but your meter still moves with every fixture off, the leak is elsewhere. That is a job for leak detection, and we can tell you which way to go after one visit.

Wax ring and fill valve: the two parts behind most repairs

The wax ring is a donut of wax that seals the toilet outlet to the floor flange. It lasts for years, but any movement of the bowl can break it. Once it fails, the toilet has to be lifted and the ring replaced, and a damaged flange gets repaired at the same time.

The fill valve is the tower inside the tank that refills it after each flush. The water level should stop about an inch below the top of the overflow tube. A fill valve that is slow, noisy, or never shuts off is cheap to swap and worth doing before it floods a bathroom.

Side by side

Fix the toilet you have, or set a new one?

Repair makes sense when

  • The bowl and tank are uncracked and sit solidly
  • The problem is a flapper, fill valve, supply line, or wax ring
  • The flush is strong and the model is still easy to find parts for
  • You like the height and shape of the current toilet

Replacement makes sense when

  • There is a crack in the tank or bowl
  • The toilet clogs repeatedly even with a clear drain line
  • Parts are discontinued or the tank is stained and pitted inside
  • You want a taller bowl, a better flush, or a dual flush upgrade
The process

How a toilet swap or new install goes

1

Shut off and drain

We close the angle stop, flush, and sponge out the last water so nothing spills on the way out.

2

Pull the old bowl and check the flange

We lift the toilet, scrape off the old wax, and inspect the flange for cracks and level. A broken flange gets a repair ring or replacement before anything is set.

3

Set the new toilet on a fresh seal

A new wax ring or a waxless seal goes down, the bowl is set straight, and closet bolts are tightened evenly so the porcelain does not crack.

4

Connect, test, and caulk

A new braided supply line goes on, we flush several times while watching every joint, and we caulk the base if you want it sealed.

Questions about toilet repair and installation in Eagle Mountain

Do you handle toilet repair emergencies at night or on weekends in Eagle Mountain?

Yes. The dispatch line never closes, so overflowing, leaking, and cracked toilets get a licensed, insured plumber on weeknights, weekends, and holidays. A same-day slot is possible when the schedule allows, and a closed angle stop limits damage while you wait.

Why does my toilet keep running after I flush?

The flapper is usually not sealing, the chain is tangled, or the fill valve is not shutting off. Drop food coloring in the tank and wait ten minutes. If color shows in the bowl, the flapper or seat needs replacing.

How do I know whether the wax ring has failed?

Water at the base, a toilet that rocks, or a faint sewer smell in the bathroom all point to it. The ring cannot be checked without lifting the bowl, so a plumber resets it and inspects the flange at the same time.

Is a toilet that keeps clogging a repair or a drain problem?

Try a plunger first. If other fixtures also drain slowly, the line is the problem, and drain cleaning is the better call. If only that toilet clogs, the trap or an older low-flow design may be the cause.

Can you install a toilet I bought myself?

Yes, as long as it is in good condition and fits the rough-in. We set it on a new seal, connect a new supply line, and test it. Please keep the box and instructions so we can check the rough-in dimension.

Do I need a permit to replace a toilet in Eagle Mountain?

Replacing a toilet in the same location is usually ordinary maintenance, but permit rules vary, so ask the Eagle Mountain Building Department at 801-789-6614. Moving a toilet or adding a bathroom is a bigger step and usually needs a permit check.

Is a leaking toilet connected to a high water bill?

It can be. A running tank loses water silently. If the bill is high and the toilet looks fine, a leak elsewhere in the house may be at work, and leak detection finds it.
