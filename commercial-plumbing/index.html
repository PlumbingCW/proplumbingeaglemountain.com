After-hours: when a business plumbing problem cannot wait

Some problems threaten the building, the stock, or the right to stay open. Call at once for water flowing through ceilings, sewage backing up from floor drains, or a main line that will not drain. Night and weekend calls are routed to dispatch, and same-day appointments are offered when available.

  1. Shut the main water valve and note where it is for the next person on shift.
  2. Turn off power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel without standing in water.
  3. Stop using restrooms, sinks, and dishwashers if drains are backing up.
  4. Move inventory, paper records, and electronics away from the water.
  5. Photograph the damage and write down the time the problem started.

Typical jobs

The calls commercial customers make most

Business plumbing problems tend to come from heavy use, shared lines, and older tenant improvements. The list below covers the calls we hear most.

Main line backups

Several restrooms or floor drains back up at once, which points to the building sewer rather than a single fixture.

Restroom fixtures

Flush valves, urinals, and toilets that run or leak in high-use restrooms wear faster than home fixtures.

Water heater trouble

A tank or commercial unit that fails leaves a kitchen or salon without hot water, and may flood the mechanical room.

Leaks in ceilings and walls

Water that appears in a lower unit often travels from a distance, so the source can be hard to find.

Floor drain odors and overflows

Dry traps, buildup, and slow lines all cause smell or standing water in utility rooms and kitchens.

Pressure and flow changes

A drop in pressure at the tap may be a partly closed valve, a failing regulator, or a hidden leak.

Honest commercial leak detection

Leak detection in a building starts with the meter. We shut off every fixture and watch the meter dial or low flow indicator, which tells us whether water is leaking at all. If it moves, we isolate zones with valves to narrow down the section, then use listening gear or thermal imaging where the pipe is concealed.

No method pinpoints every leak. A line under a slab or inside a masonry wall may need an access cut to confirm. Roof stains can also come from HVAC condensate or the roof itself, and we tell you when a problem is not plumbing. For a fuller look at the methods, see our leak detection page.

Backflow testing, a planned job

A backflow preventer is a valve assembly that stops water from flowing backward into the public supply. You often find them on irrigation, fire lines, and commercial water services. Testing checks that the check valves and relief valve hold the pressure they should.

Who requires testing, and how often, depends on the water supplier and the type of device. Ask the Eagle Mountain City Water Department what applies to your property, and give us the device make, size, and location when you call. We schedule the visit as planned work so water can be shut off briefly without disrupting business hours.

Floor drains, grease trap lines, and restaurant drains

A floor drain that smells usually has a dry trap, which is fixed by refilling it or adding a trap primer. A drain that gurgles or backs up often has a partial blockage in the branch line. In a kitchen, grease sticks to the pipe wall and narrows the line a little more each week.

We clear these lines with cables or a jet, depending on the buildup. A restaurant with a grease trap or interceptor should have it pumped and cleaned on a regular schedule, and we help you tell whether the problem is the trap or the line downstream. See drain cleaning for the methods we use on branch lines.

Questions about commercial plumbing in Eagle Mountain

Do you handle commercial plumbing emergencies at night or on weekends in Eagle Mountain?

Yes. After-hours dispatch covers leaks, backups, and failed water heaters in nonresidential buildings, handled by our dispatched plumbers. Close the main valve before you call to limit damage. A same-day appointment is possible when the schedule has room.

Who pays for a plumbing repair, the landlord or the tenant?

That depends on the lease. Generally the owner is responsible for shared lines and the building, while tenants cover fixtures they installed or damaged. We document the cause so you can settle it, but we do not decide fault.

How does commercial leak detection actually work?

We confirm a leak with the meter, then isolate zones to narrow it down. Listening gear and thermal imaging help locate concealed pipe. Some leaks need an access cut to confirm, and see leak detection for details.

How often does a backflow device need testing?

It depends on the water supplier and the device type. Ask the Eagle Mountain City Water Department what applies to your property, and give us the device details so we can plan the visit.

Why do my restaurant drains keep backing up?

Grease and food solids build up in the branch line and trap. Repeated backups mean the line needs more than a quick snake, so ask us about jetting and a cleaning schedule for the grease trap.

Can you work around business hours?

Yes. Planned work such as testing and fixture upgrades can be scheduled before opening or after close. Emergencies are handled as they come, and dispatch is open every hour.
