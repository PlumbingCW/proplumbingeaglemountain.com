Clogged drains and sewer trouble

For a single clogged sink or tub, start with drain cleaning. When a clog returns every few weeks, hydro jetting scrubs the pipe wall instead of just poking a hole. A sewer line cleaning visit clears the main line out to the street.

If several drains back up together, look at sewer line repair for broken or rooted pipes. A sewer inspection sends a camera down the line, which also helps before you buy a home.

Water heaters, from no hot water to a new tank

A tank that leaks, hisses or runs lukewarm starts with water heater repair. If the tank itself has failed, water heater replacement covers swapping it, and water heater installation is the planned version for new homes and upgrades.

Those who want endless hot water can read about a tankless water heater, including flushing and error codes. Utah water heater work may need a permit, so ask the Eagle Mountain Building Department at 801-789-6614.

Leaks, burst pipes and supply lines

A meter that moves with every tap off needs leak detection. A split or frozen pipe calls for burst pipe repair right away. If the line from the street to your home is leaking, see water service line repair.

Older or failing pipe networks may call for repiping, although most Eagle Mountain homes are newer. Call 811 before any digging for buried lines.

Fixtures: toilets, faucets and disposals

Small parts cause big messes. Toilet repair handles running tanks, leaks and clogs, while faucet repair covers drips, shower valves and supply lines. A jammed or humming unit under the sink belongs on the garbage disposal page.

Installation and specialty work

New builds and remodels need plumbing installation, including gas line safety. Businesses can read about commercial plumbing, and basements have two pages: sump pump work and basement waterproofing.

If you want to test your tap before buying treatment, read water softener. The city publishes no hardness figure, so test first.

Emergency work versus planned work

Emergency work stops damage that is happening now. The plumber fixes the urgent part first, such as closing off a split pipe, and talks through longer-term options afterward.

Planned work is scheduled with a clear price before work starts. Examples include a new water heater, a bathroom rough-in or a camera inspection. Because there is no active water loss, you can compare options calmly and ask about permits.
