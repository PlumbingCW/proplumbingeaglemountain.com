What counts as a plumbing emergency in Eagle Mountain

A plumbing emergency is any problem that is damaging your home right now or putting your family at risk. A pipe spraying water, a toilet overflowing with sewage and a water heater leaking from the tank all qualify. So does a sewer backup that shows up in a floor drain or a tub.

Call an emergency plumber when you cannot stop the water, when sewage reaches living space, or when the heat and hot water both fail in freezing weather. If you smell gas, leave the building first and call 911 or the gas utility. Phone us for the plumbing side once everyone is safe.

A slow drain, a dripping faucet or a toilet that runs after flushing can usually wait for a same-day or next-day visit. Close that fixture's supply valve if it helps and keep using the rest of the house. Phone the dispatch line if you cannot tell which group your problem falls into.

Meter still running, no hot water, slow drain back again

Most calls start with a symptom, not a part name. A meter that keeps moving when every tap is off points to a hidden leak, often a running toilet, a stuck irrigation valve or a pinhole on the line into the house. Drop food coloring in each toilet tank and wait without flushing to catch the quiet ones.

Hot water that runs out after a few minutes usually means a failed heating element, a bad thermostat or sediment in the tank. Hot water that never arrives can be a tripped breaker, a pilot that blew out or a failed gas valve. Describe the pattern on the phone and the dispatcher can plan the parts.

A slow drain that keeps coming back is a different problem. Chemical cleaners punch a hole through the clog and leave the buildup on the pipe wall. Repeat clogs usually need cabling or full-line jetting, which is why drain cleaning and hydro jetting are separate services.

While you wait: stop the water and protect the house

Your first five minutes decide how much damage the water does. Work through these steps while the dispatcher sends a plumber.

  1. Close the main shutoff valve, often near the water heater or where the line enters the garage or basement.
  2. Switch off power to wet rooms at the breaker panel, but only if you can reach it without standing in water.
  3. Open the lowest cold faucet to bleed pressure once the main is closed.
  4. Move rugs, electronics and boxes out of the wet area.
  5. Take photos of the damage for your insurance company before you clean up.

How to check a plumber's Utah license before work starts

The Utah Division of Professional Licensing (DOPL) issues plumber licenses in five classes: Apprentice, Journeyman, Residential Journeyman, Master and Residential Master. Ask any plumber at your door which class they hold and for the license number. Then search it yourself in the Verify a License lookup.

The search shows the name, class and status on record. Match the name to the person at your door and the company on the truck. A plumber who will not give a license number is one to skip, whatever the price.

Work is built to the Utah-adopted International Plumbing Code. The city's permit sheet lists water supply piping, drain-waste-vent systems, sewer connections, water heaters and major repairs, unless specifically exempted. Permit rules vary, so ask the Eagle Mountain Building Department at 801-789-6614.

Yard leaks, sewer lines and the 811 rule

A soggy patch in the yard, a green strip in dry grass or weak pressure at every tap can point to the water service line. Repairs mean digging. Call 811 (Blue Stakes of Utah) before any dig so buried utilities get marked.

We locate the leak first so the trench stays short. For sewer trouble, a camera run shows whether the problem is roots, a belly or a cracked joint, and our sewer line repair page explains the repair options.

Emergency plumbing questions in Eagle Mountain

Do you offer emergency plumbing at night and on weekends in Eagle Mountain?

Yes. Dispatch runs 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays, at (385) 488-1892. Give your address, say what is happening and close the main valve while you wait.

What should I do first when a pipe bursts?

Close the main shutoff valve, then switch off power to wet areas if you can do it safely. Open the lowest cold faucet to relieve pressure and call for a burst pipe repair.

How do I know if my water meter is showing a leak?

Turn off every tap and appliance, then watch the meter's small leak indicator or low-flow dial. If it moves, water is escaping somewhere. A toilet tank dye test is the cheapest first check, and leak detection finds the rest.

Is a leaking water heater an emergency?

A tank leaking from the base is, because a failed tank cannot be patched. Turn off the power or gas and the cold supply valve, then read about water heater replacement. A leak at a fitting is often a repair.

Will I know the price before you start work?

Yes. The plumber explains the problem and the price before work starts. Cost depends on access, parts, labor time and whether a permit applies, so call (385) 488-1892 for your real number.

Do I need a permit to replace a water heater in Eagle Mountain?

The city's permit sheet lists water heaters among work that may need a permit, unless specifically exempted. Permit rules vary, so ask the Eagle Mountain Building Department at 801-789-6614.

Why does my drain keep clogging after I pour in cleaner?

Cleaner opens a narrow channel and leaves grease and scale on the pipe wall, so the clog regrows. Cabling clears the blockage and jetting scrubs the wall. Both are options on the drain pages.
