What Temperature Should a Tankless Water Heater Be Set To in Eagle Mountain, UT?

Start a tankless water heater at 120 degrees Fahrenheit.

By the Eagle Mountain Pro Plumbing team · Published October 30, 2026 · Updated October 30, 2026

Talk to a plumber
(385) 488-1892
Answered 24/7

Start a tankless water heater at 120 degrees Fahrenheit. The US Department of Energy calls 120 degrees a temperature considered safe for most of the population. The Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC) warns that water at 140 degrees can burn skin after about six seconds of contact.

Raise the setting only if you have a specific reason, such as a fixture that never feels hot enough, and fix that problem at its source when you can. A tankless unit heats water as it flows, so the setting works a little differently than on a tank. The sections below show how to check the real temperature at your tap.

Where the 120 degree number comes from

Two federal sources point at the same figure. The Department of Energy's Energy Saver guide tells homeowners to set the water heater to 120 degrees Fahrenheit. The CPSC publication on tap water scalds says burns occur with a six-second exposure to 140 degree water, and it suggests using the lowest setting that still meets your hot water needs.

That gap of twenty degrees matters more than it sounds. At 120 degrees a brief splash is unpleasant. At 140 degrees the same splash can injure a toddler or an older adult before anyone reacts.

Worth knowing

A tankless setpoint is an outlet target, not a tank thermostat

A tank heater holds a batch of water at the thermostat temperature. A tankless unit fires its burner or element only when water moves, and it tries to hit the outlet target you pick. The work it does equals the gap between the cold water coming in and the setpoint you chose, which is called the temperature rise.

When you open many fixtures at once, flow goes up and the unit may fall short of the target. In cold months the incoming water is colder, so the same setpoint demands a bigger rise. That is why a heater can feel perfect in summer and weak in January even when the setting never changed.

Usually not the dial

Hot-then-cold swings and other complaints that feel like a bad setting

People often turn the setpoint up when the real trouble sits somewhere else. Match your symptom here before you change the number.

Water turns scalding after a cold stretch

Hot water that sat in the pipe can mix with a fresh surge. Check for an anti-scald valve and test the temperature at the tap.

Hot, then cold, then hot again

A very small draw may not trigger the unit, or a low flow may cause it to shut off. A plumber can check minimum flow and the sensor.

Never gets hot enough with two showers running

The unit may be at capacity for your cold-weather rise. A higher setpoint cannot add heating power the unit does not have.

Temperature drifts or an error code appears

Mineral scale in the heat exchanger or a failing sensor can cause drifting. Write down the code and the model before calling.

The process

How to check and adjust the setting without guessing

1

Read the current setpoint

Find the controller on the unit or the remote panel and note the number it shows. Some models list preset steps, so check the manual to see what each step means.

2

Measure at the tap

Run the hot tap for a minute, then hold a kitchen or candy thermometer in the stream. The CPSC also advises hand-testing water before bathing infants and young children.

3

Change it in small steps

Adjust a few degrees at a time, wait for the unit to settle, and measure again. Large jumps can overshoot and make the water uncomfortable.

4

Recheck after any service

After a power interruption, a cleaning, or a repair visit, confirm the setpoint is still where you left it.

Checklist

Scald safety checks for homes with children or older adults

A thermometer costs little and settles arguments about whether the water feels too hot. Run through these checks once, then repeat after any change.

Side by side

Lower setting or higher setting for a tankless unit

Staying near 120 degrees

  • Lower burn risk at every faucet
  • Slower mineral scale growth inside the heat exchanger
  • Less strain on the unit during cold-water months
  • Meets the Department of Energy guidance

Going higher

  • Can help if a far fixture feels lukewarm
  • Needs mixing or anti-scald valves to stay safe
  • Raises scald risk if anyone changes valves carelessly
  • May mask a flow or sizing problem

When the real fix is a service visit, not a new number

If the water never reaches your setting, scale on the heat exchanger or a blocked inlet filter is a common suspect. A flush clears mineral buildup, and a tankless water heater service visit also checks the vent and the error history.

If the unit repeatedly shuts down or smells of exhaust, treat it like any gas appliance and stop using it until a plumber looks. Our water heater repair crews can sort out whether the fault is a sensor, a vent, or the gas supply.

Questions readers ask

Is 120 degrees hot enough for a tankless water heater?

For most households, yes. The Department of Energy calls 120 degrees safe for most of the population, and showers, handwashing, and sinks work well there. If a dishwasher or washer seems to need more, check its manual before you raise the setpoint.

How hot is too hot for tap water?

The CPSC says burns occur with a six-second exposure to 140 degree water. The risk climbs quickly with each added degree, and children and older adults are hurt faster. That is why the lowest comfortable setting is the safest one.

Why does my tankless water heater make hot water that fluctuates?

Fluctuation usually comes from low flow, mineral scale, or a sensor problem rather than a bad setting. Run one fixture at a time to see if the swings stop. If they do not, a plumber should check the unit.

Can I set a tankless heater higher to get more hot water?

No. A higher setpoint raises the temperature, not the volume. If two showers run short, the unit may be too small for your cold-weather conditions, and a plumber can tell you whether a different model fits.

Should I test my water temperature after the first cold snap?

Yes. Utah winters bring hard freezes, and colder incoming water means a bigger temperature rise. A quick thermometer check tells you if the unit still reaches your setpoint at normal flow.

What should I ask the Eagle Mountain Water Department before choosing a tankless heater?

Ask for the annual drinking water quality report and ask which sources feed your street. The report does not give a hardness number, so test your tap yourself. That result tells you how often to plan a descaling flush.
