Why home age matters on these streets

Eagle Mountain incorporated in 1996, so any house in The Ranches dates from the late 1990s or later. That rules out galvanized supply pipe and cast iron for almost every home, but it does not rule out worn parts. Tank water heaters, shutoff valves, toilet fill valves and faucet cartridges all wear out on their own schedule.

A tank that came with an early house may now be far older than the 8 to 12 years often quoted for storage heaters. Moisture at the base, rust streaks or a tank that rumbles are reasons to look at water heater replacement before the tank fails on its own.

Questions from The Ranches homeowners

Do you take plumbing calls in The Ranches?

Yes. Call (385) 488-1892 and give the street, the cross street and a main road such as Ranches Parkway. Dispatch confirms the address before sending anyone.

Are Ranches homes old enough to have galvanized pipe?

Almost certainly not, since the town incorporated in 1996 and the master plan dates to 1998. We did not verify the pipe material on any specific street, so a plumber should identify it at your main before you plan a repipe.

Does replacing a water heater here need a permit?

The city permit sheet lists water heaters, though exemptions exist. Call the Eagle Mountain Building Department at 801-789-6614 with your address and ask before work begins.

How do I know my water heater is near the end of its life?

Rust at the base, moisture on the floor, popping or rumbling sounds and a tank that no longer keeps hot water for long are the usual signs. Age matters too, and the date is on the sticker.

How can I check a plumber before letting them in?

Search the plumber's name on the state Verify a License lookup and compare the class and status with what they told you. Utah issues apprentice, journeyman and master classes.
