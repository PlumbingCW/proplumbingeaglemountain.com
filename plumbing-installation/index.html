We serve Eagle Mountain and nearby towns, and same-day appointments are offered when available for walkthroughs. Every job gets a clear price before work starts.

Plumbing installation means connecting water supply, drain, vent and gas lines to a fixture or appliance so it works safely and passes inspection. Done right, the work behind the wall matters as much as the fixture you see.

Worth knowing

Smell gas? Leave the building first

If you smell gas, a rotten egg odor or hear a hiss near a gas line, leave the building right away. Do not turn lights on or off, do not use a flame or a phone inside, and do not try to find the source.

From outside, call 911 or your gas utility before you call a plumber. We cannot promise a response time for a gas smell, because the utility and fire department are the right first responders and they have the tools to make the building safe.

After the gas is shut off and the utility says the building is clear, we can inspect, repair or install gas piping. Gas line work requires a permit and a pressure test.

New construction plumbing: rough-in, top-out and final

In a new house, plumbing happens in stages. Rough-in sets drain, waste and vent pipes, supply lines and stub-outs before walls close. A pressure test and inspection follow, and the final stage installs fixtures and appliances once finishes are done.

Eagle Mountain has grown from a small town in the 1990s into a large city, and new homes keep going up. If you are a builder or an owner-builder, bring the plan set and we will confirm fixture counts, pipe sizes and venting before the first cut.

Common fixture installs

Sinks, tubs, toilets and appliances we set

Fixtures look simple. Supply, drain and venting all have to be right behind the wall.

Kitchen sink and faucet

Supply stops, a proper trap and a drain tied into the vent system.

Toilets and bidets

A level flange, a new wax or rubber seal and correct supply hookups.

Showers and tubs

Valve rough-in at the right depth, a sound drain and a tested pan or liner.

Washer and dishwasher hookups

Supply, drain standpipe or air gap and shutoff valves.

Water heater connections

Expansion control, relief piping and a sound connection to venting or power.

Outdoor bibs and utility sinks

Frost-resistant bibs and drains that will not freeze and split.

Remodels where the plumbing has to follow the design

A remodel changes where water, drains and vents need to go. Moving a toilet a few feet may require a new drain path and a vent change. Adding an island sink means running a drain across a floor with the right slope.

We review the design with you before demo so the cabinet maker and tile setter work to the right dimensions. If your new layout reveals leaking or tired supply lines, we can discuss repiping while walls are open, and our water heater installation work can happen in the same project.

Questions about plumbing installation in Eagle Mountain

What should I do if I smell gas?

Leave the building at once and call 911 or your gas utility from outside. Do not use switches, flames or phones indoors. A plumber comes after the utility has made the building safe.

Can you promise how fast you will respond to a gas smell?

No. The utility and the fire department are the first responders for a gas smell. We repair or install gas piping once the area is safe.

Do I need a permit to install a new faucet or toilet?

Replacing in the same location is often treated as ordinary maintenance, but permit rules vary. Ask the Eagle Mountain Building Department at 801-789-6614.

Do new construction homes need plumbing inspections?

Yes, in stages. Rough-in plumbing is tested and inspected before walls close, and the final inspection follows fixture installation.

Can you move a toilet or add a sink to my layout?

Usually yes, but it affects drains and vents. We check slope, vent size and access before agreeing to a layout.

Does installing a gas appliance need more than a connection?

Yes. Gas piping needs sized lines, a leak test and often a permit. See water heater installation for gas-fired tank requirements.

How do I check my plumber's license?

Use the Verify a License lookup from the Utah Division of Professional Licensing. It lists apprentice, journeyman, residential journeyman, master and residential master licenses.
