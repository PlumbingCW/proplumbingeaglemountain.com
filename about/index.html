How to verify any Utah plumber license, ours included

The Utah Division of Professional Licensing (DOPL) issues plumber licenses in five classes: Apprentice, Journeyman, Residential Journeyman, Master and Residential Master. An apprentice works under supervision, while journeyman and master classes can work independently at different levels. Residential classes cover residential work.

Ask the plumber for the name on the license and the license number. Open the Verify a License lookup, enter the details and read the class and status shown. If the record does not match the person in front of you, stop the job and call us or the state.

Eagle Mountain water and what it means for your pipes

Eagle Mountain City Water Department supplies the city from five groundwater wells plus surface water from the Central Utah Water Conservancy District. The city's water quality report does not publish a hardness figure, so we do not quote one here.

If you want to know how your own water behaves, test your tap with a simple hardness kit or ask the Water Department. The result tells you whether scale is likely to shorten the life of a water heater or clog fixtures, which is useful before you pick a repair or a replacement.

Most homes here are newer, so the pipes are often younger than in older Utah towns. Age is not the only cause of trouble, though, and a plumber still has to look at the actual problem instead of assuming.

Codes, permits and digging rules we follow

Plumbing in Utah is built to the Utah-adopted International Plumbing Code, with state amendments. The Eagle Mountain Building Department lists permits for water supply piping, drain-waste-vent systems, sewer connections, water heaters and major repairs, unless specifically exempted. Permit rules vary, so ask the Building Department at 801-789-6614 about your project.

Before any digging, such as a water service line repair, call 811 so Blue Stakes of Utah can mark buried utilities. Our water service line repair page explains how locating and trenching fit together.
