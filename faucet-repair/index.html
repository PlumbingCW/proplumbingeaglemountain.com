Faucet repair in Eagle Mountain, UT, start to finish

Water that sprays from a broken faucet or cannot be shut off at a stuck valve is an emergency, and dispatch is open 24/7. Find the main shutoff, usually where the water line enters the home, and turn it clockwise until the flow stops. Then call and tell us what failed.

Faucet repair is the fixing of the handles, cartridges, valves, and supply connections that control water at a sink, tub, or shower. In Eagle Mountain, UT we repair or replace the fixture, the supply valves under it, and the shower valve behind the wall.

When a valve will not shut off or a supply line gives way

A fixture shutoff that will not close leaves you with only one way to stop the water, which is the main valve. A burst supply line under a sink can empty a lot of water in a short time, so move fast. After hours or on a weekend, call the dispatch line and a licensed plumber will schedule the first available visit.

  1. Turn off the main water valve and open a low faucet to relieve pressure.
  2. Move items out of the cabinet under the sink and set a bucket under the leak.
  3. Switch off power to nearby outlets if water is near them, and do not stand in standing water.
  4. Photograph the damage for insurance before you wipe anything up.
  5. Do not force a stuck valve with pliers, since a snapped stem makes the leak worse.

The kitchen and bathroom faucet itself

Most modern faucets use one of four designs: a cartridge, a ball, a ceramic disc, or the older compression washer. Each fails in its own way. A cartridge faucet drips when the seal cracks, a compression faucet drips when the rubber washer hardens, and a ceramic disc faucet drips when grit scores the disc.

Repair beats replacement when the body is solid and parts are still sold. If the finish is pitting, the spout is loose, or the model is discontinued, a new faucet costs less in the long run than another repair visit.

Worth knowing

Why the little valve under the sink matters

Every sink, toilet, and washing machine should have its own shutoff, called an angle stop or straight stop. Older ones use a multi-turn washer stem that can leak at the handle or freeze in place. A quarter turn ball valve closes with a short twist and is much less likely to seize. Swapping these during a faucet job means the next repair does not require shutting off the whole house.

Shower valve, showerhead, and tub faucet problems

A showerhead that sprays sideways or trickles is often clogged with mineral deposits and can be soaked or replaced. A shower that changes temperature when someone flushes a toilet points to a failing pressure balance cartridge. A tub faucet that drips or will not divert to the shower usually has a worn diverter or cartridge.

Most shower valves live behind a tile wall. We check whether the cartridge can be reached through the trim, which avoids cutting into tile. If the valve body itself leaks, an access panel from the back side is often the least damaging route.

The process

How a faucet or valve repair visit unfolds

1

Stop the water

We close the local shutoff or the main valve and open the fixture to drain the line, so nothing sprays when parts come off.

2

Identify the brand and failure

The cartridge, seat, or O-ring is matched to the exact model. A mismatched part often drips again within weeks.

3

Replace, clean, and reseat

We swap worn parts, clean mineral buildup from the body, and replace the aerator or supply lines if they are cracked or stiff.

4

Test under pressure

We run hot and cold at full flow, check every joint with a dry paper towel, and confirm the shutoff valves close completely.

Slow shower and tub drains that keep coming back

A tub that fills at your ankles or a shower pan that holds water is usually a hair and soap clog in the trap or the branch line. A plunger or drain snake clears the first. If the slow drain returns within weeks, the line may have scale or a sag. Drain cleaning clears the line, and hydro jetting scours it when snaking only buys a short fix.

If a tub drain leaks into the ceiling below, stop using it and call. A failed overflow gasket or a drain shoe leak can soak drywall quickly.

Questions about faucet, valve and fixture repair in Eagle Mountain

Do you handle faucet repair emergencies at night or on weekends in Eagle Mountain?

Yes, around the clock. Valves that will not shut off, burst supply lines, and spraying shower valves count as urgent, and licensed, insured plumbers take those calls at any hour. Close the main shutoff while you wait. A same-day visit may fit if the schedule has room.

Why won't the shutoff valve under my sink turn?

Mineral crust or a corroded stem can lock it in place. Forcing it can snap the stem. A plumber replaces the valve, often with a quarter turn ball valve, once the main is closed.

Is a dripping faucet worth repairing?

Yes. A drip wastes water, stains the basin, and signals a worn part that will fail further. Many repairs are a cartridge or washer swap, and replacement is the better path only if the faucet body is damaged.

How do I know if my shower valve needs replacing?

Temperature swings, a handle that sticks or turns too far, or a leak behind the wall suggest a failing cartridge or valve body. A plumber starts with the cartridge. A full valve replacement comes up only if the body is corroded or leaking.

Can you fix a tub faucet that leaks only when the shower is on?

Usually the diverter is worn, which sends water out the spout instead of the shower head. It is a small repair when the diverter is reachable, and the cartridge or spout is replaced if it is not.

What if the shower drain is slow after you fix the faucet?

That points to a clog in the trap or branch line. We can clear it with a snake, and hydro jetting is an option if the clog keeps coming back.

Who should replace the supply line under a bathroom sink?

Any plumber can, and many homeowners do it. Use a braided stainless line sized for your valve, and replace the line when you replace the faucet. Do not reuse old lines with new faucets.
