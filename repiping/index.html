Repiping in Eagle Mountain, UT: when new lines beat more patches

Repiping in Eagle Mountain, UT means replacing the water supply lines inside your home with new pipe, either throughout the house or along the runs that keep failing. It is a planned project, and same-day appointments are offered when available for the walkthrough.

A repipe replaces the pipes that carry pressurized hot and cold water to your fixtures. It does not touch the drain lines, which are a separate system. Most jobs use PEX or copper, and the work is pressure tested before walls are closed.

Signs the pipes are the problem

Repeat leaks, spotty pressure and stained walls

People rarely say they need a repipe. They say the same pipe keeps leaking. The plumbing name for the solution is repiping, and these are the symptoms that lead there.

A second or third pinhole leak

One pinhole is bad luck. Several in the same home usually mean the pipe or its conditions are the issue.

Leaks at fittings, not pipe

Failed joints and fittings point to installation problems, not to wear.

Pressure fading over time

Narrowed or damaged supply pipe cuts flow to showers and appliances.

Stains on ceilings and walls

Repeated damp patches near plumbing walls are a warning even after the drywall is patched.

Water that tastes or smells off

Take a sample to a lab first. A plumber can check for pipe-related causes but does not diagnose health effects.

Remodel plans

Moving a kitchen or bathroom is the cheapest moment to replace the lines behind the walls.

Why a newer city still has pipes that need replacing

Most Eagle Mountain homes were built since the late 1990s, so age is rarely the reason. Pipes fail in newer houses because of high water pressure, rushed joints, damaged tubing, corrosion from the outside of a pipe and fittings that were never seated properly.

Galvanized pipe is unlikely in homes of this era, so a repipe here is usually about a specific failure pattern. We would rather prove the pattern with a pressure test than replace pipe that is working fine.

Repair the leak or repipe the house

Repairs still make sense when

  • One pinhole or fitting failed and nearby pipe tests sound
  • The leak was caused by a freeze or impact
  • The pipe is accessible and the fix is a short section
  • You plan to sell soon and want the lowest cost fix

A repipe makes sense when

  • Leaks keep appearing in different places
  • Pressure tests show weakness across several runs
  • Fittings are failing in batches
  • Walls are already open for a remodel

Remodels and moved fixtures are the best time to repipe

A kitchen remodel or bathroom addition often exposes pipes that would otherwise stay hidden. New fixtures, a relocated sink or an island with a prep faucet all need supply lines routed in new directions.

While walls are open, replacing the nearby old runs costs a fraction of opening them later. If you are planning that work, our plumbing installation team can coordinate fixture rough-in and supply lines in one visit.

Questions about repiping in Eagle Mountain

How do I know if I need a repipe or just a repair?

One leak with healthy pipe nearby is a repair. Several leaks in different spots, failing fittings or weak pressure tests suggest a repipe. A pressure test and camera look settle the question.

My pipes keep leaking in a house built in the last 25 years. Is that normal?

It is less common but it happens. High pressure, bad joints, damaged tubing and outside corrosion all cause early failures. Finding the pattern matters more than the pipe's age.

Is my house plumbed with galvanized pipe?

Unlikely in a home built since the late 1990s. A plumber can confirm in minutes by looking at the pipe entering the water heater or the main valve.

Will repiping tear up my walls?

It requires access cuts, but PEX can be fished through walls with small openings. Open attics and basements reduce the number of cuts.

How long will the water be off?

It depends on house size and the plan, and we set that schedule during the walkthrough. Ask whether water will be back on at the end of each work day.

Does repiping need a permit in Eagle Mountain?

Likely yes for a whole house job. The city permit sheet lists water supply piping, but permit rules vary, so ask the Eagle Mountain Building Department.

Can I repipe in stages while remodeling?

Yes. Many homeowners replace the kitchen and bath runs first. Ask us about leak detection first if you are unsure which runs are failing.
